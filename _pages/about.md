Policy researcher who applies a single discipline — rigorous quantitative policy evaluation paired with
legislative translation and international coordination — to the **resilience of critical national
systems**. Trained as a health policy researcher (PhD candidate, George Washington University; dissertation
defended November 2026), I built that methodology
studying the health workforce, then extended it to the defense industrial base as Deputy Director of the
National Security Program at Taiwan's DSET. The same analytic toolkit that produced peer-reviewed
workforce research (JAMA Network Open, Health Affairs Scholar; 165 citations, h-index 6) also produced
the flagship UAV supply-chain report *Drones for Democracy*, cited in Bloomberg, The Economist, and
Deutsche Welle. IVLP Fellow (U.S. Department of State) and AcademyHealth Board member. My work advances
U.S. national interest by strengthening the resilience of the systems the country depends on — its
health workforce and its defense supply chains.

Research interests
------
Resilience of critical systems: health workforce (interstate/international mobility of nursing and
healthcare professions, systemic health inequality) and defense industrial base (UAV supply chains,
allied cooperation); quantitative policy evaluation; comparative public health.

One method, two critical systems
------
Health workforce and defense supply chains are not two unrelated skill sets; they are two applications of
one method. In both, I ask the same question — *how does a critical system withstand shocks, and which
policy levers build resilience?* — and answer it with the same tools:

- **Quantitative policy evaluation** (difference-in-differences, event studies, econometrics) — used to
  measure how the nurse licensure compact shifts interstate mobility *and* to assess fragility in UAV
  component supply chains.
- **Legislative translation** — eight years turning technical evidence into enacted law and policy
  options in Taiwan's parliament, applied equally to health, labor, environmental, and defense bills.
- **International coordination** — liaison work with WTO/ADB and allied-partner engagement on U.S.–Taiwan
  defense cooperation.

The health policy doctoral training supplies the formal methodological foundation; the national-security work is
that foundation deployed on a second critical system, backed by a flagship report, sustained media
authority, and a standing think-tank appointment.
